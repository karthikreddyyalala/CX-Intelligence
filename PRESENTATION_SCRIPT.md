# CX Intelligence — Full Presentation Script
_Plain English. Read top to bottom. ~18 minutes._

---

## BEFORE YOU OPEN THE SCREEN (say this first, ~3 min)

> "Before I show you anything, I want to start with one line from our own 10-K.
>
> Under Customer Service, it says we track satisfaction by **sending surveys to recent customers**. That is our listening program — we email people after their rental and ask them to rate us.
>
> Here's the problem. Across the industry, only about **1 in 6 people** ever answer a survey. And the angriest customers are exactly the ones who don't answer — they don't want to fill out our form, they go straight to Google and write a public review instead.
>
> Same 10-K, in the risk factors: **'Damage to our reputation or brands may negatively impact our business.'** We told our shareholders that reputation is a material risk. But the only thing watching that risk is a survey that 5 out of 6 customers ignore.
>
> So I built something that reads the other 5 out of 6. Let me show you."

**Then open the dashboard. Don't rush. Let the top load.**

---

# CHAPTER 1 — THE GAP
_"What the survey programme cannot see"_

### Section 01 — The Blind Spot
**What's on screen:** big number 1,780, then 30 branches / 251 staff / $17,674 / 0 from surveys.

**Say:**
> "This is everything customers said about Avis that a survey never captured. 1,780 reviews, 30 branches, and not one of them came from a survey — every single one is a customer who went out of their way to post publicly. This is the blind spot. It already exists, it's public, it's about us — and today nobody is systematically reading it."

### Section 02 — Dispute Radar
**What's on screen:** a ranked list of complaints that name a dollar amount, each with a risk score 0–100.

**Say:**
> "Now I filter to just the complaints about money. 54 customers publicly named a dollar amount they say we wrongly charged them — $17,674 in total. I rank them by how likely they are to escalate into a bank chargeback or a lawsuit.
>
> Here's the top one." **(Read the $1,410 one out loud.)**
> "This customer paid for full damage waiver, got charged $1,410 anyway, and — in their own words — has **already filed a dispute with Chase Bank**. That's not a prediction. That's a customer telling us, in public, that the chargeback is already happening. And 21 of these 54 wrote their booking reference number right in the review — we could look up their rental and fix it in minutes."

**If they push "is nobody replying?" — be honest:**
> "No, we do reply — to 75% of these. But 90% of the replies ask the customer to go email socialinfo@avis.com and re-send their reservation number, name, phone, email — things many of them already put in the review. The complaint gets acknowledged. The charge stays disputed. That's the gap this closes."

# CHAPTER 2 — THE EVIDENCE
_"What customers are actually saying"_

### Section 03 — Executive Pulse
**What's on screen:** the KPI panel — overall rating, sentiment mix, source breakdown.
**Say:** "This is the corpus behind every number so far — every review from all 5 sources, scored, with a confidence indicator so you know how solid each figure is."

### Section 04 — Ask (the live analyst)
**What's on screen:** a text box where you type a question.
**Say:** "This isn't a static report. Ask it anything in plain English — 'what are people saying about the JFK counter?' — and Claude answers from the actual reviews and quotes the proof." **(Type one live question if the room is engaged.)**

### Section 05 — AI Executive Brief
**Say:** "A leadership-ready summary of sentiment health and what to fix first — written by Claude from the real reviews."

### Section 06 — Top Complaints, Ranked
**Say:** "The recurring themes dragging the brand down, ranked by how negative and how frequent they are. This is your fix-first list."

### Section 07 — The Business Case
**What's on screen:** revenue-at-risk calculator + response rate.
**Say:** "This translates complaint volume into dollars. You set your own assumption for what a customer is worth, and every figure updates. It's how you turn 'people are unhappy' into a number finance understands."

---

# CHAPTER 3 — INTELLIGENCE
_"Where it's happening, and who's involved"_

### Section 08 — Location Intelligence
**What's on screen:** 30 branches ranked worst-first.
**Say:** "The same brand performs 30 different ways. Here's every branch on its own reviews, worst first. Click any one and you see the exact complaints behind its score. This tells you where to send help — it's not the whole network, it's specific branches."

### Section 09 — People Intelligence
**What's on screen:** staff names customers mentioned.
**Say:** "Customers name names. This surfaces who's earning goodwill — the staff getting praised by name. It's designed recognition-first."
**(For a leadership room, keep this brief. Don't dwell — naming staff can read as surveillance.)**

### Section 10 — Identity Intelligence
**Say:** "One angry person can post on every platform under a different name, making one bad day look like four complaints. This finds the likely duplicates — same story, same day, same branch — and AI rules on each one, showing its reasoning. It keeps the numbers honest."

### Section 11 — Platform Intelligence

### Section 12 — Trends Over Time

### Section 14 — Live Feed
**Say:** "Every raw review, filterable. This is the ground truth — everything above is derived from what's in here. Nothing is made up."

### Section 15 — Weekly Email
**Say:** "One click turns all of this into a leadership email. That's how it becomes a Monday habit, not a tool people forget to open."

## THE ASK (close with this, ~1 min)

Don't ask for budget. Ask for a person:

> "I'd like 30 minutes with whoever owns billing disputes today. I want to check whether the complaints I'm flagging match the cases they're already fighting. If they do, this becomes an early-warning system for a cost we're already paying — turning a $45M problem back into a $250 phone call while it's still cheap to fix."

---

## HOW THIS IS DIFFERENT FROM SPROUT (if asked)

> "Sprout is the mailbox — it's where you publish posts and reply to messages. My tool is what tells you **which** of those thousand messages actually matter — which 54 are about money, which branch is worst, which complaint is about to become a chargeback. Sprout can't rank by risk or pull out a dollar amount. It's not a replacement — it's the intelligence layer Sprout doesn't have."

---

## HARD QUESTIONS — HAVE THESE READY

**"Is $17,674 the real exposure?"**
> "It's the floor. It only counts complaints where the customer typed a dollar figure — most don't. And when someone lists several amounts, I count only the largest. The real number is higher; I'd rather understate it in this room."

**"How do you know these complaints are true?"**
> "I don't verify them against our books — these are customer claims, not confirmed charges. But 21 include their own booking number, so anyone with system access can check them in minutes."

**"Isn't the risk score subjective?"**
> "The weighting is mine. The inputs aren't — the dollar amount, the star rating, and the exact words are read straight from the review. You can disagree with my weighting without disagreeing with a single fact on screen."

**"Don't we already do this?"**
> "You reply to reviews — 75% of the negative ones, and fast. What's missing isn't the reply, it's the ranking. Nobody today can tell you which complaint is most likely to become a lawsuit, or how much money is being disputed in public this month. This can."

**"Why 2026 so much bigger than other years?"**
> "Two reasons — Google surfaces recent reviews more completely, and this is when I built the collection. So recent months are the most complete. I'm not claiming complaints are exploding — just that the current picture is solid."
